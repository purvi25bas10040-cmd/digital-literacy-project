Phishing is one of the most common cybercrimes today, especially among students who frequently use email and online services. In a typical phishing attack, the victim receives an email or message that looks like it is from a trusted source such as a bank, university, or popular website. The message usually creates a sense of urgency, like asking the user to verify their account or update their details.
For example, a college student might receive an email claiming to be from their bank, stating that their account will be blocked if they do not verify their details immediately. The email includes a link that looks genuine but actually leads to a fake website. When the student enters their login credentials or OTP, the attacker gains access to their account.
Phishing usually targets people who are not aware of such scams or who act quickly without verifying the source. The consequences can be serious, including loss of money, personal data theft, or even identity misuse.
This type of cybercrime works mainly because attackers copy the design and language of real organizations, making it difficult to distinguish between real and fake messages. Awareness and careful checking are the best ways to avoid falling into such traps.
